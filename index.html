CHAPTER XIV • BURIAL CLOTHS & RELICS

Two Shrouds: Turin & Manoppello

Transpositioning of the Sacred Faces

Two images of Jesus' face from the Shrouds of Turin and Manoppello are so remarkably congruent that optical transpositioning is possible. Recent research confirms 1st-century provenance and distinct features on both sides of the Manoppello byssus fabric, affirming the Gospel accounts of the resurrection.

EXCAVATION COIN CATALOG • 1ST-CENTURY JUDEA & EMPIRE
01

Prutah minted by Herod Antipas

GALILEE & PEREA • TIBERIAS MINT

Bronze prutah struck under the tetrarch Herod Antipas at Tiberias. Bears the palm branch symbol and Greek inscription 'TIBEPIAC' honoring Emperor Tiberius.

DENOMINATION: BRONZE PRUTAH • REIGN: 4 BCE – 39 CE
Obverse & Reverse Diagnostic Plate
02

Coin of King Aristobulus V and Queen Salome

ARMENIA MINOR / CHALCIS

Extremely rare bilingual royal issue depicting Aristobulus V on the obverse and Queen Salome (the dancer from Matthew 14:6 and Mark 6:22) on the reverse with her full royal title.

DENOMINATION: BRONZE COIN • DATE: C. 56–57 CE
Aristobulus & Queen Salome Plate
03

Roman Denarius with a head of Tiberius Caesar

IMPERIAL ROME • THE TRIBUTE PENNY

On this image is a gold coin (Aureus). The silver equivalent of this coin — the Denarius (bearing the exact same images on both sides: portrait of Emperor Tiberius on the obverse and seated Livia as Pax on the reverse) — was the coin used by Jesus in the Gospel discussion about paying taxes to Caesar ('Render unto Caesar the things that are Caesar's', Mt 22:15–22).

DENOMINATION: SILVER DENARIUS • LUGDUNUM MINT • C. 14–37 CE
Gold Aureus (Denarius Equivalent) • Tiberius & Livia
04

Coin of Herod the Great

JUDEAN KINGDOM • JERUSALEM / SAMARIA

Official bronze issue of King Herod the Great. Features pagan-neutral architectural and ceremonial motifs complying with Jewish aniconic sensitivities: helmet, tripod table, and pomegranate.

DENOMINATION: 8 PRUTOT / BRONZE • REIGN: 37–4 BCE
Tripod & Helmet Plate
05

Coins of Pontius Pilate from AD 26, AD 30 and AD 31

PROCURATORIAL JUDEA • LITUUS & SIMPULUM

The providential series struck in Jerusalem during Pilate's prefecture (Years 16, 17, and 18 of Tiberius). Boldly features pagan Roman augural instruments—the lituus (augur's wand) and simpulum (libation ladle)—provoking Judean religious outrage.

DENOMINATION: BRONZE PRUTAH • DATED LIS (AD 29/30), LIZ (AD 30/31), LIH (AD 31/32)
AD 29/30 • Simpulum
AD 30 • Lituus (LIZ)
AD 31 • Lituus (LIH)
06

Coin of Philip the Tetrarch

ITURAEA & TRACHONITIS • CAESAREA PHILIPPI

Minted at Caesarea Philippi (Banias) by Herod's son Philip. The first Jewish ruler to depict the bust of Roman Emperors (Augustus and Tiberius) and the pagan Augusteum temple at the spring of Pan.

DENOMINATION: BRONZE ISSUE • REIGN: 4 BCE – 34 CE
Augusteum Temple Plate
